## A note on how this book was made

This book was researched, drafted and checked with the help of an AI model made by Anthropic. The same company's products, Claude Code and Claude Cowork, appear in sources discussed in Chapter 19, which deals with AI coding tools and the Indian software industry. Readers should weigh what the book says about AI with that in mind. The products are described there as the sources describe them, and the book makes no claim about them beyond what those sources say.

The work followed a fixed method. A register of sources was compiled first, and every chapter was drafted only from research dossiers built on that register. Each chapter was then checked by a separate pass that re-opened the sources behind every quotation, number, date, name and causal claim, corrected or softened what did not hold, and logged the result. A revision pass removed repetition and contradictions across chapters. The checking logs, the source register and the data files are kept in the book's public repository.

## A note on sources

No one was interviewed for this book. Every person who speaks in it does so through a published or archived record: a memoir, an oral history, a company filing, a court judgment, a parliamentary record, a government report, a scholarly study, or a newspaper or magazine report. The notes give the source and, where one exists, the page.

Some people appear only under names given to them by others. Where a reporter or a scholar used a pseudonym, the book uses the same one and says so where the person first appears. No character in the book is a composite, and no scene, thought or line of dialogue has been invented.

Quotations are exact and kept short. Where a source could be read only in summary, the book paraphrases it rather than quoting. Figures published by companies, by the industry's lobby group Nasscom, or by consultancies that sell to the firms they count are labelled as theirs. Disagreements between sources are shown rather than resolved where the evidence does not settle them. Material published after mid-2025 was checked against the live source in October 2026, and the notes say so.

Ordinary workers speak less often in this book than its subject deserves. The published record of Indian software work is dominated by founders, executives, officials and scholars; the accounts of trainees, testers, call-centre agents and engineers who never became prominent are few, and most are anonymous or pseudonymous. Where such accounts exist, the book uses them and labels them. Where they do not, the gap is part of the story.

Rupee amounts are converted to US dollars at the average rate for the year in question, and Indian fiscal years, which run from April to March, are written in the form FY2023-24. Cities are given the names in use at the time, with the current name at first mention in each chapter.
