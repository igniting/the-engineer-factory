## 1. TMA Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka (Supreme Court, 31 October 2002)

- **Citation:** *T.M.A. Pai Foundation & Ors. v. State of Karnataka & Ors.*, Writ Petition (Civil) 317 of 1993 and connected matters, Supreme Court of India, eleven-judge bench, judgment of 31 October 2002 (reported (2002) 8 SCC 481). Lead (majority) opinion by B.N. Kirpal CJI; separate opinions by V.N. Khare, S.S.M. Quadri, Ruma Pal and S.N. Variava JJ (with Ashok Bhan J).
- **URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/512761/
- **Accessed:** 2026-10-15 (full text read via Indian Kanoon HTML; the page does not print the SCC citation, which is taken from the register's existing reference)
- **Kind:** primary (judgment)
- **Bears on:** private engineering colleges after 2002 (Chs 7, 12); fees and admissions.

Facts and holdings (paragraph numbers are the majority opinion's):

1. Unni Krishnan's scheme of free and payment seats overruled. Para 45: "we hold that the decision in Unni Krishnan's case, insofar as it framed the scheme relating to the grant of admission and the fixing of the fee, was not correct, and to that extent ... [is] overruled."
2. The court described that scheme as nationalisation. Para 38: "The scheme in Unni Krishnan's case has the effect of nationalizing education in respect of important features, viz., the right of a private unaided institution to give admission and to fix the fee."
3. Karnataka figures used to show private colleges as a necessity. Para 39: "out of 51 Engineering Colleges, only 12 have been established by the government."
4. Fees in unaided institutions. Para 56: "The decision on the fee to be charged must necessarily be left to the private educational institution that does not seek or is not dependent upon any funds from the government."
5. But no profiteering or capitation fees. Para 57: "There can, however, be a reasonable revenue surplus, which may be generated by the educational institution for the purpose of development of education and expansion of the institution."
6. Management quota for private unaided professional colleges. Para 68: "a certain percentage of the seats can be reserved for admission by the Management out of those students who have passed the common entrance test held by itself or by the State/University". The rest go through state counselling, and the government fixes the percentage "according to the local needs".
7. Para 69: "A rational fee structure should be adopted by the Management, which would not be entitled to charge a capitation fee."
8. Para 70: "It is well established all over the world that those who seek professional education must pay for it."
9. Answers to the eleven questions (Q.5(c)): "Fees to be charged by unaided institutions cannot be regulated but no institution should charge capitation fee." Q.9: the Unni Krishnan scheme "is unconstitutional. However, the principle that the[re] should not be capitation fee or profiteering is correct. Reasonable surplus to meet cost of expansion and augmentation of facilities does not, however, amount to profiteering."
10. Q.11: the right to establish and administer educational institutions is "guaranteed under the Constitution to all citizens under Articles 19(1)(g) and 26, to minorities specifically under Article 30."

Note: the answers to the questions are printed twice in the Indian Kanoon text (once after the lead opinion and once in a separate opinion); the wording above is from the first set. Later cases (Islamic Academy 2003, P.A. Inamdar 2005) narrowed or clarified TMA Pai; these were not read in this pass.

## 3. H-1B fee: *State of California v. Mullin* (D. Mass., 8 June 2026)

### 3a. Memorandum and Order (Doc. No. 106)

- **Citation:** *State of California et al. v. Markwayne Mullin et al.*, Civil No. 25-13829-LTS (D. Mass.), Memorandum and Order on Plaintiffs' Motion for Summary Judgment (Doc. No. 86) and Defendants' Cross-Motion for Summary Judgment and Motion to Dismiss (Doc. No. 92), Sorokin J., 8 June 2026, 42 pp. Reported as 833 F. Supp. 3d 50 (per the N.D. Cal. order, item 4).
- **URL:** https://clearinghouse-umich-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/doc/172282.pdf (Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse doc 172282; case page https://clearinghouse.net/case/47504)
- **Accessed:** 2026-10-15 (full text; pp. 1-2, 26, 37-42 read closely)
- **Kind:** primary (court order)
- **Holdings:**
  - Plaintiffs: "twenty states that have sued various executive departments and officials for their role in implementing the Proclamation" (p. 1).
  - p. 26: "the Court finds that the Policy imposes a tax on H-1B petitions without the requisite delegation by Congress." Summary judgment for the states on the separation-of-powers claim (Count IV).
  - The court also granted the APA claims and chose vacatur over an injunction. p. 41: "the Court VACATES the Policy materials implementing the Proclamation's $100,000 payment requirement." It also issued a declaratory judgment but no separate permanent injunction.
  - p. 42 (Conclusion): "The Policy implementing the Proclamation is declared unlawful and is VACATED in its entirety." Claims against DOJ/Attorney General and Labor/Secretary of Labor were dismissed without prejudice.
  - Vacatur is not limited to the parties. p. 40 n.13: "vacatur is a remedy targeted at an agency's unlawful action, rather than one limited to the particular parties in a given case." The court also rejected a claim-preclusion argument based on *Chamber of Commerce v. DHS*, 815 F. Supp. 3d 73 (D.D.C. 2025).

### 3b. Judgment (Doc. No. 107), 8 June 2026

- **URL:** https://clearinghouse-umich-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/doc/172283.pdf; the same document is mirrored at https://www.adhikarilaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/State-of-California-et-al-v.-Noem-et-al-1-25-cv-13829-Massachusetts-District-Court_Judgement_Adhikari-Law-PLLC.pdf (the mirror was the copy read)
- **Accessed:** 2026-10-15 (full, 2 pp.)
- **Kind:** primary
- **What was vacated, exactly:** "The following agency actions are VACATED pursuant to the Administrative Procedure Act and declared unlawful insofar as they impose a $100,000 payment obligation on H-1B petitions". Ten items are listed:
  - (i) CBP memorandum from Matthew S. Davies, 20 Sep 2025;
  - (ii) Bureau of Consular Affairs email to the Visa Office, 19 Sep 2025;
  - (iii) its action request, 20 Sep 2025;
  - (iv) State Department "H-1B FAQ", 21 Sep 2025;
  - (v) State Department webpage "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers", 21 Sep 2025;
  - (vi) USCIS memorandum on the Proclamation, 20 Sep 2025;
  - (vii) USCIS "H-1B FAQ", 21 Sep 2025;
  - (viii) USCIS "H-1B Specialty Occupations" webpage, 15 Oct 2025;
  - (ix) USCIS G-1055 Fee Schedule, 20 Oct 2025;
  - (x) USCIS H-1B visa payment website, 20 Oct 2025.
- **Resolves dispute aaa:** the court vacated the agency **implementing policies** ("the Policy"), not Proclamation 10973 itself. Law-firm summaries saying "the Proclamation was vacated" (S0137, Clark Hill) are inaccurate on this point.

## 4. H-1B fee: *Global Nurse Force v. Trump* (N.D. Cal., 30 September 2026)

- **Citation:** *Global Nurse Force, et al. v. Donald J. Trump, et al.*, Case No. 25-cv-08454-HSG (N.D. Cal.), Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Plaintiffs' Motion for a Preliminary Injunction, and Denying Motion for Class Certification and Motion to Dismiss (Dkt. No. 130), Haywood S. Gilliam Jr. J., filed 30 September 2026, 35 pp.
- **URL:** https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.457426/gov.uscourts.cand.457426.130.0.pdf
- **Accessed:** 2026-10-15 (pp. 1-3, 19-21, 32-35 read; rest searched)
- **Kind:** primary (court order)
- **Holdings:**
  - Scope disclaimer, p. 2: "this case is not about whether the challenged Proclamation is good or bad".
  - p. 2 records the other cases. *Chamber of Commerce* (D.D.C.) "upheld the Proclamation and its $100,000 fee", and that case is on appeal to the D.C. Circuit. In *California v. Mullin*, the D. Mass. court "declared unlawful and vacated the Department of Homeland Security's and the Department of State's policies implementing the $100,000 fee. 833 F. Supp. 3d 50, 60-84 (D. Mass. 2026)". "The First Circuit later denied the government's request for a stay pending appeal. See California v. Mullin, 183 F.4th 42, 46-52 (1st Cir. 2026)".
  - Merits: plaintiffs are likely to succeed on the claims that the Agency Policies are arbitrary and capricious and were adopted without notice-and-comment rulemaking (pp. 28-32).
  - p. 33: the court "GRANTS IN PART Plaintiffs' motion for a preliminary injunction, to the extent they seek vacatur of the Agency Policies. The Court otherwise DENIES the motion at this time."
  - p. 34 (Conclusion): "The Agency Policies are VACATED and REMANDED to Agency Defendants. Agency Defendants ... are enjoined from enforcing or implementing the Agency Policies made pursuant to or in implementation of Proclamation No. 10973 and its extension in Proclamation No. 11069, until notice-and-comment rulemaking under the APA and regulatory flexibility analyses under the RFA have been conducted."
  - No bond; stay pending appeal denied. Motion to dismiss denied. Class certification denied without prejudice, because "the Mullin court already vacated the Agency Policies such that the $100,000 fee is no longer in effect, and the First Circuit declined to stay the vacatur pending appeal" (p. 34).
  - Case management conference set for 27 October 2026.
- **Appeal:** Dkt. No. 138, filed 9 Oct 2026, is the Ninth Circuit's notice assigning appeal no. 26-6667 (*Global Nurse Force v. Trump*) on the preliminary-injunction time schedule. The government has appealed. URL: https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.457426/gov.uscourts.cand.457426.138.0.pdf
- **Note:** this is a preliminary-injunction order, not a final judgment. It vacates and enjoins the agency policies; it does not strike down the Proclamations themselves.

## 5. CAG performance audit of UIDAI (Report No. 24 of 2021)

- **Citation:** Comptroller and Auditor General of India. *Performance Audit on Functioning of Unique Identification Authority of India*, Report No. 24 of 2021, Union Government (Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology). 108 pp. PDF. Audit period 2014-15 to 2018-19, with statistics updated to March 2021. Search results say it was tabled in Parliament in April 2022 (not verified in the document).
- **URL:** https://d7i5wg8xwe4hf.cloudfront.net/uploads/download_audit_report/2021/24%20of%202021_UIDAI-0624d8136a02d72.65885742.pdf (linked from https://cag.gov.in/en/audit-report/details/116042). The cag.gov.in chapter-1 URL in S0128 now returns 404; per-chapter files are on the same CloudFront path.
- **Accessed:** 2026-10-15 (full text extracted; front matter and chs 1, 3 read)
- **Kind:** primary (audit report)
- **Facts:**
  - p. 1: "Unique Identification Authority of India was created on 28 January 2009 as an attached office of the then Planning Commission".
  - p. 2: "the ambitious Aadhaar Scheme was launched on 29 September 2010 in Tembhli, a village in Nandurbar district of Maharashtra ... The Aadhaar database has since reached 129.04 Crore by March 2021".
  - Executive summary, p. v (PDF p. 11): UIDAI staff were "130 at its Delhi Headquarters and ... 219 at its Regional Headquarters at the end of March 2021", "mostly either on deputation or from outsourced agencies". The 2020-21 budget was Rs 613 crore against actual expenditure of Rs 892.67 crore.
  - p. 19 (para 3.2.2): "nearly 4.75 Lakh duplicate Aadhaar numbers were cancelled as of November 2019. This data indicated that on an average no less than 145 Aadhaars generated in a day during the period of nine years since 2010 were duplicate".
  - Executive summary, p. vi (PDF p. 12): "There were instances of issue of Aadhaars with the same biometric data to different residents indicating flaws in the de-duplication process". On Bal Aadhaar for children under five, UIDAI "incurred avoidable expenditure of `310 Crore on issue of Bal Aadhaars till 31 March 2019".
  - Vendors: UIDAI has agreements with three ABIS (biometric de-duplication) vendors selected by the Managed Service Provider (ch. 3). M/s Wipro Ltd appears as Data Centre development vendor in the contracts table (ch. 5).
