## 1. CHM oral history of Arjun Malhotra (HCL co-founder), 2018

- **Citation:** Computer History Museum. 'Oral History of Arjun Malhotra.' Interviewed by Uday Kapoor and Douglas Fairbairn, recorded 15 November 2018, Mountain View, CA. CHM Reference X8840.2019; transcript catalogue 102740453 (video 102740454, 1:52:27). 37 pp.
- **URL:** https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/chm-cms-media/media-live/_file/import/102740453-05-01-acc.pdf (catalogue record for video: https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102740454)
- **Kind:** primary (oral history; founder's self-account, recorded 40+ years after events).
- **Read:** full text pp. 1-27 read; pp. 28-37 skimmed by keyword.
- **Facts bearing on open questions:**
  - *Microcomp vs HCL (not a rename; two entities).* p. 13: "we ended up owning 74 percent and this 49% was held in the original calculator trading company, Microcomp." Same page: "Once we got the licence to manufacture computers we incorporated Hindustan Computers Limited in August 1976. It's now called HCL." So Microcomp was the founders' calculator-trading firm that held the public tranche of the new joint-sector company; HCL was incorporated separately. The name Microcomp came from DCM's programmable calculator (p. 13).
  - *Joint sector with UP state.* p. 13: "UPTRON owned 26 percent. We the promoters owned 25 percent, and 49 percent was supposed to be offered to the public."
  - *Founding year and capital.* p. 9: "Fairbairn: So what year was this? / Malhotra: 1975." p. 10: "We pooled in all our money, six of us. We had 175,000 rupees". (Note: other accounts give Rs 187,000.)
  - *Imports.* p. 9: "Kapoor: So, there was a restriction on importing computers, but not chips? / Malhotra: Yeah. Components were allowed."
  - *First computer.* p. 14: "We got the computers out only in '77. And the product-- the first machine was based on the Rockwell PPS-8. It was called the HCL8/C." Aimed to replace IBM 1401s (p. 14).
  - *Software import rule.* p. 16: "we technically weren't allowed to import software unless it was in source code form ... which is why UNIX became so popular in India".
  - *Market size.* p. 15-16: competitors DCM and ORG each selling "about 200 computers a year"; HCL later "doing 1,000 computers a year" (p. 16).
  - *CMC as protected rival (mid-1980s).* p. 23: "the government had a public sector company, CMC, that was selling imported plug compatible mainframes from East Germany."
  - *HCL in the US and the origin of its services arm (body-shop-type billing).* p. 20: "HCL still has the same office in Sunnyvale that we took in 1989." p. 21: hardware team laid off; engineers placed with software vendors "because they were paying me $45+ an hour in those days. I'm going back to 1989." p. 23: "That is how HCL Technologies started."
  - *Applications vs engineering.* p. 23: "engineers were for R&D. Programmers were to write applications."
  - *Techspan (1999).* p. 27: "We were just selling contract programmers to do this."
  - **IBM's exit is not discussed** in this transcript.

## 2. CHM oral history of Natarajan Chandrasekaran (TCS), 2020

- **Citation:** Computer History Museum. 'Oral History of Natarajan Chandrasekaran.' Interviewed by Uday Kapoor, audio recorded 15 January 2020, Mumbai. CHM Reference X9236.2020; catalogue 102792002. 19 pp. (Footer misprints every page as 'Page 17 of 17', same fault as S0036; cite PDF page.)
- **URL:** https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/chm-cms-media/media-live/_file/import/102792002-05-01-acc.pdf (record: https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102792002/)
- **Kind:** primary (oral history; current Tata Sons chairman, company insider).
- **Read:** pp. 1-10 in full; rest by keyword.
- **Facts:**
  - *Joining TCS, 1986-87; Burroughs machine still in use.* PDF p. 5: "I applied to TCS in '86 ... They gave me two assignments, one was in Burroughs mainframe, which is a Burroughs 5900 machine."
  - *Recruitment scale c. 1987.* PDF p. 5: "I am talking about TCS of about 500- 600 professionals. And they used to hire 20 people or 40 people a year." Also: "they would go to ITT Bombay or ITT Delhi and they would recruit the whole class" (transcript spells IIT as 'ITT').
  - *Machine cost vs pay.* PDF p. 4: "the cost of a machine was 10 times more than the compensation of the individual per year."
  - *TCS size c. 1987 and IBM 3090.* PDF p. 5: "We were 27 crores. That's about $8M in those days"; capital cost of the IBM 3090 order "was higher than the revenue of TCS."
  - *Interviewer's framing (not evidence):* PDF p. 5: TCS "first started with Lalit Kanodia and with Mr. F. C. Kohli".
  - *Training.* PDF p. 6: experienced staff "had to make at least 20 days a year for training".
  - *Y2K.* PDF p. 8: "the Year 2000 opportunity brought to bear: One, it gave us scale".
  - *'10B by 2010'.* PDF p. 9: "I think it was 2002, we were about 600M- 700M or so."

## 3. CHM oral history of Nandan Nilekani, 2020 (title page says 2019)

## 4. CHM oral history of N.R. Narayana Murthy, Part 1, 2018 (resolves S0035)

- **Citation:** Computer History Museum. 'Oral History of N. R. Narayana Murthy' (Part 1). Interviewed by David C. Brock and Uday Kapoor, recorded 13 May 2018, Mountain View, CA. CHM Reference X8632.2018; transcript catalogue 102740329 (video 102740330). 16 pp.
- **URL:** https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102740329-05-01-acc.pdf
- **Kind:** primary (oral history; founder's self-account). This is the item the register lists as S0035 'unverified'; it can be upgraded.
- **Read:** pp. 7-15 in full.
- **Facts:**
  - *IIT Kanpur computers.* PDF p. 7: "It had two computers - IBM 7044, and IBM 1620."
  - *Softronics and IBM's exit (chronology problem).* PDF p. 14: Softronics is placed in "1975, '76" by the interviewer; Murthy: "That was the time when IBM and Coca-Cola had walked out of India because of a certain restriction that the government had placed in their equity structure." IBM left in 1978, so either Softronics is later than 1975-76 or Murthy's memory compresses events. Do not use as evidence for the exit date.
  - *Patni business model.* PDF p. 14: "I was invited by the Patnis to become the general manager of software. They had decided to represent Data General computers in India. They had also started a business to develop software for export to the United States." Group of "about 100 youngsters". "I was there for about four years before I started Infosys."
  - *Infosys founding date.* PDF p. 15: "The company was founded on 7 July 1981."
  - *First Infosys export work.* PDF p. 15: CAMP package (New York client) upgraded to DG MV/8000 and rehosted on an IBM 4341.

## 6. Computerwoche, 14 April 1978: IBM's exit as reported at the time

- **Citation:** '(ee)'. 'ICL und Ostblockrechner stoßen in eine Lücke: IBMs provozierter Rückzug bringt Indien nichts als Ungemach.' *Computerwoche* (Munich), 14 April 1978. Dateline New Delhi/Köln; draws on *Nachrichten für Außenhandel*.
- **URL:** https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2885076/icl-und-ostblockrechner-stossen-in-eine-luecke-ibms-provozierter-rueckzug-bringt-indien-nichts-als-ungemach.html
- **Kind:** primary (contemporary trade press, German). Full text read. Translations below are the agent's.
- **Facts:**
  - *Exit timing and cause.* Para 1: "stellt bis Ende 1978 in Indien sämtliche Aktivitäten ein" (will end all activities in India by the end of 1978); FERA would have allowed IBM "nur noch mit 40 Prozent an seiner indischen Tochter" (only 40 per cent of its Indian subsidiary).
  - *Installed base.* Para 5: "Auch scheint nicht völlig klar zu sein, wie sich der Ersatz der 150 IBM -1400 vollziehen wird." (It is not clear how the 150 IBM 1400s will be replaced.) Supports 'over 150' for the 1400-series base alone; says nothing about CMC maintaining 800.
  - *CMC as new monopolist, higher charges.* Para 1: CMC "als Monopolist auf dem von IBM aufgegebenen indischen Markt herrscht". Para 4: CMC reportedly charges fees "die noch über denen der IBM lägen" (above IBM's).
  - *Electronics Commission's role.* Para 2: the confrontation "möglicherweise bewußt herbeigeführt worden" (possibly deliberately brought about) by the Electronics Commission; IBM said it had offered in 1970 to replace the 1400s with 360s and in 1972 with 370s.
  - *Replacement plan.* Para 4: ECIL and ICL each to build "100 Computer mittlerer Größe" over five years; demand forecast c. 300 systems to 1981-82.
  - *Software exports.* Para 9: "Software wird bereits exportiert. Firmen wie Tata Consultancy Services, Bombay, und Operations Research Group, Baroda ... 1977 dürfte diese Ausfuhr rd. 10 Millionen indische Rupien erreicht haben." (1977 software exports about Rs 10 million.)
  - Says 'as CW reported several times': earlier 1977-78 Computerwoche items exist but were not found.

## 12. The Economist, 'Ram drive' (21 August 2008): Ramadorai, Burroughs and the export obligation

- **Citation:** 'Ram drive: The career of Subramanian Ramadorai of Tata Consultancy Services parallels the rise of India's software industry.' *The Economist*, print edition, 21 August 2008 (Face Value column; unsigned).
- **URL:** mirror on a Pace University course page, https://webpage.pace.edu/pviswanath/articles/aeg4e43/india/ramadorai_tcs.html (original economist.com URL not checked; cite the Economist issue, note the mirror).
- **Kind:** secondary press drawing on interviews with Ramadorai, Pendharkar and Mahalingam (company insiders). Full text read.
- **Facts:**
  - *Ramadorai's start.* Para 1: "joined Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) ... in 1972".
  - *Burroughs import, 1974, and duty.* Para 2: "On the new Burroughs computer that TCS bought in 1974, it paid a tariff of 101.25%, including import duty, auxiliary duty, countervailing duty and a levy to help pay for the war in Bangladesh."
  - *Import-for-export rule (tied to that import).* Para 4: "The government required TCS to earn twice as much from exports as it spent on its imported machines." Supports a 1974 date for TCS's own obligation; it does not say when the general rule began.
  - *Burroughs relationship.* Para 4: "It won its first foreign contracts with the help of the Burroughs corporation, then the number two computer-maker, which sold its machines in India through TCS."
  - *Early offshore work by post.* Para 4: Mahalingam on a project in Feltham, outside London: instructions mailed "two copies, one day apart."
  - *Computer time vs pay.* Para 5: "One hour's computer time cost more than a programmer earned in a month."
  - *First overseas office, 1979.* Para 6: "In 1979 Mr Ramadorai ... was sent back to America to open the company's first overseas office. TCS had just left Burroughs's marsupial pouch".
  - *Group's entry.* Para 2: "The Tata group first ventured into computers in 1968, serving its own companies with second-hand IBMs and an early ICL machine."
