## S0064 *Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka* (SC, 30 July 1992). Full text read (Indian Kanoon). Pages from (1992) 3 SCR 658.

### Law

- The Karnataka Educational Institutions (Prohibition of Capitation Fee) Act, 1984 replaced Ordinance No. 14 of 1983. It came into force with effect from 11 July 1983 (p. 663).

### Fees

Karnataka notification of 5 June 1989 under s. 5(1), for private medical colleges:

| Category | Tuition fee per year |
|---|---|
| 'Government seats' | Rs 2,000 |
| Karnataka students | up to Rs 25,000 |
| 'Indian students from outside Karnataka' | up to Rs 60,000 |

### Facts (pp. 663-64)

- Mohini Jain, 'a resident of Meerut', was told by Sri Siddhartha Medical College, Agalkote, Tumkur, that she could join the MBBS course beginning February/March 1991.
  - She had to deposit Rs 60,000 for the first year and a bank guarantee for later years.
- 'The petitioner's father informed the management that it was beyond his means to pay the exorbitant annual fee of Rs.60,000 and as a consequence she was denied admission to the medical college.'
- She alleged a further demand of 'capitation fee of rupees four and a half lakhs'. The management 'vehemently denied the same'.

### The colleges' case (pp. 664-65)

- Karnataka private medical colleges get no state aid.
- They spend about Rs 5 lakh per student over 5 years.
- 40% of seats are government seats at Rs 2,000.
- Counsel argued that higher fees from 'non-meritorious' students were a valid classification.

### Held (headnote)

- 'Capitation fee is nothing but a price for selling education. The concept of "teaching shops" is contrary to the constitutional scheme and is wholly abhorrent to the Indian culture and heritage.' [670C]
- 'The capitation fee brings to the fore a clear class bias.' [673F-G]
- 'A poor student with better merit cannot get admission because he has no money whereas the rich can purchase the admission.' [673F-G]
- The right to education 'flows directly from right to life' (Art. 21) [669F-G].
- The Rs 60,000 'is not tuition fee but in fact a capitation fee' [681C].

### Relevance

The case concerned a medical college, but the Karnataka Act and the reasoning covered all private professional colleges, engineering included. The engineering petitioners in *Unni Krishnan* said Mohini Jain would force them to close (S0063).

## S0063 *Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (SC, 4 February 1993). Full text read. Pages from (1993) 1 SCR 594.

Page markers were taken from the Indian Kanoon text. Headnote pages are cited as 'headnote'.

### Who was in court

- Petitioners ran 'medical/engineering colleges in the States of Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu'.
- They said that if Mohini Jain were followed they 'will have to close down; no other option is left to them'.

### State laws upheld

The Karnataka Act 1984, the Maharashtra Act 1987 and the Tamil Nadu Act 1992 were all held constitutional (headnote).

### Andhra Pradesh (pp. 636-37)

- 'There are a number of private engineering colleges in the State.'
- Until 1992-93 all their seats were filled by the convenor of the common entrance test. The colleges could charge 'a particular fees which was relatively higher than the fees charged in the Government Engineering Colleges'.
- After Section 3-A was inserted in the 1983 Act on 15 April 1992, the colleges claimed the right to admit 50% 'according to their choice, irrespective of merit'.
- The court's comment: 'It is obvious that such a stand meant collection of capitation fee as much as they could.'
- 'There was an uproar among the student and teaching community'.
- The convenor allotted only 50% of seats, 'thereby sending an explicit signal that the colleges were free to fill up the rest on their own'.
- The previous year's permitted fee was Rs 10,000 a year: 'We are told that the fees permitted to be collected was Rs. 10,000 per annum' (p. 691).

### Maharashtra (p. 639)

- Uniform fee of Rs 6,500 a year in private unaided engineering colleges, raised to Rs 8,500 in 1991.
- In 1992, Rs 17,000 for students from outside the state.

### Tamil Nadu

- A medical college petitioner said it collected Rs 4 lakh each from 75 of 125 students, and that this was 'hardly sufficient'.
- The Governor's ordinance had prohibited capitation fees.

### AICTE (Union's submission, p. 673)

- 'so far as engineering colleges are concerned, permission is being granted by the A.I.C.T.E. subject to the condition that they do not collect any capitation fee'.
- AICTE guidelines let technical colleges recover 'only a graded percentage of the average cost of student education'.

### NPE 1986 as quoted

'commercialisation of technical and professional education will be curbed. An alternative system will be devised to involve private and voluntary effort in this section of education'.

### The arguments

- Private colleges: a 'right to establish a self-financing educational institution, which institution might also be described as one providing cost-based education' (headnote, petitioners' argument).
- Opponents: 'a capitation fee did not cease to be a capitation fee just because it was called as cost-based education or by calling the Institution concerned as a self-financing Institution' (headnote, respondents' argument).

### Held

- Mohini Jain was wrong to say the right to education flows from the right to life without limit. Citizens cannot demand 'that the State provide adequate number of medical colleges, engineering colleges and other educational institutions to satisfy all their educational needs' (p. 655).
- Right to free education up to age 14.
- Private unaided professional colleges may charge more than government colleges, within a ceiling. 'commercialisation of education' is not permissible (headnote).
- The scheme from 1993-94 (headnote; pp. 685-688):
  - single state notification and common admission;
  - 50% 'free seats' filled by merit at government fee;
  - 50% 'payment seats' filled by merit among those willing to pay the fixed higher fee.
- Andhra students admitted by management in 1992-93 could continue. Colleges had to charge them only the 'government fee' and remit excess to government (p. 691).

### Context

Later displaced by *TMA Pai* (2002). Ch. 12.

## S0075 Maharashtra Educational Institutions (Prohibition of Capitation Fee) Act, 1987 (Act VI of 1988). Preamble and s. 2 read as images.

- Assent 13 April 1988; published 15 April 1988; amended 1996.
- Preamble:
  - 'the practice of collecting capitation fee for admitting students into educational institutions ... is on the increase in the State';
  - 'this undesirable practice has been contributing to large scale commercialisation of education which is not conducive to the maintenance of educational standards';
  - 'the National Policy on Education 1986 envisages that the commercialisation of technical and professional education should be curbed'.
- s. 2(a): '"capitation fee" means any amount, by whatever name called ... in excess of the prescribed or, as the case may be, approved, rates of fees regulated under section 4'.

## S0076 AICTE Act 1987. Full text read (Indian Kanoon).

### Long title

'proper planning and co-ordinated development of the technical education system throughout the country, the promotion of qualitative improvements of such education in relation to planned quantitative growth and the regulation and proper maintenance of norms and standards in the technical education system'.

### Definitions

- s. 2(g) 'Technical education': 'programmes of education, research and training in engineering, technology, architecture, town planning, management, pharmacy and applied art and crafts and such other programme or areas the Central Government may ... declare'.
  - Computer applications and software are not named.
- s. 2(h) 'technical institution': 'an institution, not being a university which offers courses or programmes of technical education'.

### Functions (s. 10)

- (a) survey, collect data and 'make forecast of the needed growth';
- (j) 'fix norms and guidelines for charging tuition and other fees';
- (k) 'grant approval for starting new technical institutions and for introduction of new courses or programmes';
- (n) 'take all necessary steps to prevent commercialisation of technical education';
- (o) admission guidelines;
- (p) inspection;
- (q) withhold grants;
- (u) set up a National Board of Accreditation.

### Inference to test

Short computer courses by private companies (NIIT, Aptech) probably fell outside 'technical education' unless notified. Confirm before stating.
