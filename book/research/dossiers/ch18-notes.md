## 1. The swing in hiring (company filings)

| FY2021 | 1,427,618 | 135,216 | 48,029 |
| FY2022 | 5,866,636 | 523,385 | 222,500 |
| FY2023 | 5,342,299 | 389,183 | 114,374 |
| FY2024 | 2,436,929 | 194,367 | 26,975 |

Infosys employees at 31 March:
- 2020: 242,371 (228,449 in delivery);
- 2021: 259,619;
- 2022: 314,015;
- 2023: 343,234;
- 2024: 317,240 (a fall of about 26,000).

Offers rose 4.6 times from FY2021 to FY2022, then fell by 88% from FY2022 to FY2024. This is the sharpest swing in the factory's history (compare FY2008-09, offers 46,300 to 16,900: Ch. 14).

Cognizant (S0293):
- 'In 2021, we experienced unprecedented attrition, which was considered industry-wide. As a result, we hired over a hundred thousand new employees'.
- 10-K FY2023: 'we hired over a hundred thousand new employees in each of 2021 and 2022, and over sixty thousand in 2023'.
- Attrition including voluntary and involuntary: 20.6% (2020), 30.8% (2021), 31.7% (2022). Annualised Q4: 19.0% (Q4 2020), 34.6% (Q4 2021), 25.3% (Q4 2022).
- 'Our attrition is weighted towards our more junior employees. In 2021, voluntary attrition constituted the vast majority of our attrition'. In 2020 voluntary attrition was only about half, 'as our personnel actions taken under our Fit for Growth Plan' increased involuntary exits.
- New metric 'Voluntary Attrition - Tech Services': 25.6% (2022), 13.8% (2023).
- 'The rate of attrition began to decrease in the second half of 2022'.
- Headcount: 289,500 (end-2020), 330,600 (2021), 355,300 (2022; 258,500 in India), 347,700 (2023; 254,000 in India).
- 'While we strive to adjust pricing to reduce the impact of compensation increases on our operating margin, we may not be successful' (10-K FY2021).

Wipro (S0292): 'more than 175,000, 200,000 and 240,000 employees' at March 2020, 2021 and 2022; 'more than 240,000, 250,000 and 234,000' at March 2022, 2023 and 2024.

## 2. Remote work

Infosys:
- FY2020 20-F: 'By March 20, 2020, we had enabled 93% of our employees to be able to work remotely.'
- FY2021: 'As of March 31, 2021, we had enabled 99.3% of our employees with a secure remote working environment and 96.5% of our employees were working remotely.'
- FY2022: 'we were able to transition 99% of employees globally to a work from home arrangement' at the onset.
- Risk in FY2020: 'Our ability to continue to deliver service delivery obligations while our employees work from home are sometimes constrained by contractual terms with our clients'.
- Risk in FY2021: 'The productivity of our employees may be negatively impacted due to isolated remote working from home, quarantine requirements, negative social sentiment and personal anxiety.'
- From FY2023: 'Our transition to a hybrid working model may expose us to various risks.'

Point: the offshore model had depended on secure client-dedicated floors (Ch. 11: 'secure areas' and satellite links). Within weeks it moved to homes. That proved the work could be done anywhere, which undermined the campus model and made moonlighting possible.

## 3. Moonlighting

**S0131 (BT, 21 September 2022).**
- Rishad Premji at the AIMA convention: 'The reality is that there are people today working for Wipro and working directly for one of our competitors and we have actually discovered 300 people in the last few months who are doing exactly that.'
- He called it 'complete violation of integrity in its deepest form'.
- His tweet in August 2022: 'chatter about people moonlighting in the tech industry'; 'it is cheating- plain and simple.'
- Infosys had sent a 'strong and firm message to employees last week that moonlighting is not allowed'.
- N. Ganapathy Subramaniam (TCS COO): employees 'might lose out in the long term'.
- T.V. Mohandas Pai: 'No, moonlighting is not cheating.' 'Employment is a contract between an employer who pays me for working for them for 'n' number of hours a day. ... Now what I do after that time is my freedom'.

**Infosys 20-F FY2023 and FY2024 (S0290, S0291).** 'Continued remote working exposes the company to the risk of some employees working for multiple employers simultaneously (moonlighting) without obtaining required prior approvals and this may result in potential conflict of interests, confidentiality breach, and reduced productivity.'

NITES called the Wipro terminations illegal (register note). Primary not read.

**Thesis link.** Moonlighting exposed the time-based contract (Ch. 9 and 11). If the employer sells the employee's hours to clients, the employee's other hours become contested property. Pai's view, 'n number of hours a day', is the time-and-materials logic applied to the employee.

## 4. Unions

**KITU (S0132 Mondaq; S0294 TNM).**
- 'On 7th November 2017, the Commissioner of Karnataka Labour Department certified the formation of the Karnataka State IT/ITES Employees Union (KITU) under the Trade Union Act, 1926'. It 'claims to have about 250 IT industry employees as its members' (S0132).
- Mondaq calls it 'the first trade union in India, officially registered and recognised by the labour department, which is dedicated to the information technology ("IT") sector'. A law firm's characterisation; 'first' is to be checked.
- Mondaq: the Industrial Disputes Act's prior approval for retrenchment 'does not cover IT industry'. By notification of 25 January 2014, Karnataka exempted IT companies from the Standing Orders Act for five years.
- Foundation conference, 20 August 2017, about 200 people at YWCA Hall, Koramangala (S0294).
- Vineeth, 'who works as a testing lead with MindTree and is the General Secretary': 'if an employee is facing a problem, they have to fight in their individual capacity and the companies may not listen to them.'
- Sethumadhavan, 'a Wipro employee': 'Today the industry is facing so many challenges - from layoffs to automation. This is the right time to form an organisation'.
- Amanullah Khan, president: 'Trade unions are generally seen as anti-social or anti-national. ... But we are not individuals anymore.'
- Mohandas Pai and others 'have opposed the idea of a union for the IT sector' (TNM's characterisation).

**NITES** (Nascent Information Technology Employees Senate), Pune-based (S0295):
- 20 August 2024: its president, Harpreet Singh Saluja, filed complaints over Infosys's delayed onboarding of 'over 2,000 fresh engineering graduates' recruited in 2022-23 as System Engineers and Digital Specialist Engineers.
- 3 September 2024: the Union Labour Ministry directed the Karnataka Labour Commissioner to act (directive signed by Regional Labour Commissioner O.P. Singh).
- Infosys had issued about 2,000 final offer letters with joining dates in October 2024. About 700 were still awaiting joining dates (S0295).

**February 2025 (S0133, DH).**
- Infosys 'admitted to laying off over 300 freshers, who had undergone foundational training at the Infosys Mysuru campus for months'. NITES said 700.
- NITES complaint: 'Infosys Ltd has resorted to forcibly terminating recently onboarded campus recruits who had already suffered a delay of two years in their joining after being issued offer letters.'
- Infosys said freshers must clear internal assessments 'within three attempts, failing which they will not be able to continue', a process 'in existence for over two decades'.
- The Union Labour Ministry directed Karnataka to take 'urgent necessary actions'.

Point: this links to Mysore (Ch. 12) and the 14-week training. The factory's assessment gate became the instrument of exit.

## 5. Delayed and revoked offers (S0134, BT, 5 October 2022)

Anonymous freshers:
- 'I was placed in Accenture as Associate Software Engineer (ASE) via campus placement in November 2021. I received my offer letter in the last week of April this year. My onboarding is being pushed time after time, the latest date was September 30 and now it has been pushed to somewhere in 2023.'
- From CV Raman Global University, Bhubaneswar: 'The college placement cell should have helped us in this tough time'.
- 'I had packed my bags and booked all tickets to move to the new city, just two days before joining, they postponed the date again.'
- 'I am riddled with depressive feelings because I rejected TCS, Wipro, and HCL for a job in Accenture.'
- 'It's a year since I have been waiting to get onboarded. It is going to reflect as a gap year in my CV'.

BT says it had earlier reported delays at Wipro, Infosys, HCL Tech, Tech Mahindra and Capgemini.

Parallels: 2001-02 (Ch. 11, Anna University), 2009 (Ch. 14, Satyam: Kavitha). The third time in the factory's history that offers outran need.

## 6. Thesis links

- **Remote work** proved the location of work was negotiable, which weakens the campus and city logic (Ch. 10, 12) and prepares the ground for AI: if the work can be done from anywhere by anyone, it can increasingly be done by software.
- **The hiring swing** (offers x4.6 then -88%) shows how much the factory's intake depends on clients' discretionary spending. The 2023-24 slowdown overlapped with the arrival of generative AI. Causation is not established; the chapter must not claim that AI caused the 2023-24 hiring fall without evidence.
- **Unions** arose where the industry's legal exemptions (Standing Orders, ID Act) met mass layoffs and deferred joiners.
