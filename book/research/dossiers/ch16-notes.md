## 1. The confession (S0122)

Dated 7 January 2009, to the board of Satyam Computer Services. Balance sheet as of 30 September 2008:
- 'Inflated (non-existent) cash and bank balances of Rs.5,040 crore (as against Rs. 5361 crore reflected in the books)';
- 'An accrued interest of Rs. 376 crore which is non-existent';
- 'An understated liability of Rs. 1,230 crore on account of funds arranged by me';
- 'An over stated debtors position of Rs. 490 crore (as against Rs. 2651 reflected in the books)'.

September quarter 2008: reported revenue Rs 2,700 crore and operating margin Rs 649 crore (24%), 'as against the actual revenues of Rs. 2,112 crore and an actual operating margin of Rs. 61 Crore ( 3% of revenues)'.

How it grew:
- 'What started as a marginal gap between actual operating profit and the one reflected in the books of accounts continued to grow over the years.'
- 'It was like riding a tiger, not knowing how to get off without being eaten.'
- The Maytas acquisition 'was the last attempt to fill the fictitious assets with real ones.'

Raju's claims in his defence:
- No shares sold in eight years except for philanthropy.
- Rs 1,230 crore was 'arranged to Satyam ... to keep the operations going by resorting to pledging all the promoter shares'.
- 'Every attempt was made to keep the wheel moving and to ensure prompt payment of salaries to the associates.'
- 'neither me, nor the Managing Director took even one rupee/dollar from the company'. These are claims; the courts and SEBI found otherwise on gains (S0123).
- 'None of the board members, past or present, had any knowledge'.

Scale: 'I have seen it grow from few people to 53,000 people, with 185 Fortune 500 companies as customers and operations in 66 countries.' 'I sincerely apologize to all Satyamites and stakeholders'.

The register notes press reproductions differ from the SEC copy ('accrued' vs 'acquired'). Use the SEC copy. Raju's lawyers later disputed authorship (register). Note this when drafting.

## 2. The first days (S0279)

- **7 January 2009, Satyam press release.** Interim CEO Ram Mynampati: 'We are obviously shocked by the contents of the letter.' The company aims 'to protect the careers and security of its approximately 53,000 associates'.
- **DSP Merrill Lynch to SEBI, 7 January.** It terminated its advisory engagement on 6 January: 'In the course of such engagement, we came to understand that there were material accounting irregularities'. This is the outside trigger that preceded the letter by a day.
- **8 January.** The Special Judge for Economic Offences, Hyderabad, on a petition filed 'at 04.30 PM at my residence', authorised the Registrar of Companies to search and seize records.
- **SEBI** appointed an investigating authority (Ex. 99.1, dated 7 January).
- **9 January.** Government decision to nominate up to 10 directors; current board suspended (press release). The Company Law Board order (CP No. 001 of 2009, Union of India v. Satyam) was issued the same day. The release speaks of '53,000 employees and their families, 300,000 shareholders and 650 customers'.
- **13 April 2009.** The board selected Venturbay Consultants, a Tech Mahindra subsidiary, as the highest bidder. Bids: Rs 58 (Tech Mahindra), Rs 45.90 (Larsen & Toubro), Rs 20 (WL Ross consortium) per share. Process under Justice S.P. Bharucha. Kiran Karnik, chairman: the selection 'signals a new stage'. 'The process to select a strategic investor has reached this significant stage within three months of the new Board's first meeting.'

## 3. The auditors and the regulators

**SEC (S0280, 5 April 2011).**
- Five PW India affiliates (Lovelock & Lewes, Price Waterhouse Bangalore, Price Waterhouse & Co. Bangalore, Price Waterhouse Calcutta, Price Waterhouse & Co. Calcutta) paid a '$6 million penalty, the largest ever by a foreign-based accounting firm in an SEC enforcement action'.
- They were barred from new US clients for six months.
- 'Satyam agreed to settle fraud charges, pay a $10 million penalty'.
- PCAOB: $1.5 million penalty from two affiliates.
- Findings: PW India staff 'failed to conduct procedures to confirm Satyam's cash and cash equivalent balances or its accounts receivables'; 'routinely relinquished control of the delivery and receipt of cash confirmations entirely to their audit clients'.
- Robert Khuzami: 'PW India violated its most fundamental duty as a public watchdog'.
- 'Since the fraud came to light, the India government seized control of the company by dissolving its board of directors'.

**SEBI (S0123, order of 16 October 2018).**
- The SEBI order of 15 July 2014 held the Raju brothers and others liable under the SEBI Act, the PFUTP Regulations and the insider-trading regulations.
- SAT (12 May 2017) 'upheld the findings ... on merits' but remanded disgorgement and debarment.
- The 2018 order recomputes gains for three officials. Debarment for V. Srinivas (former CFO) and G. Ramakrishna: 7 years. Interest at 12% a year from 7 January 2009.
- Cites 'order of CBI Special Court dated April 09, 2015'.
- Register (S0124, press): Raju, his brother and eight others convicted, seven years' rigorous imprisonment. The judgment is not read.

## 4. Employees (S0281, Business Today, 8 February 2009)

- T. Sreedhar (TMI Network, recruiter): employees 'stuck between the devil and the deep sea. They cannot quit the job because there is no option being made available by the large companies (who have chosen not to hire them) and while staying back, they are not certain if they will get the salaries due to them this month'.
- NASSCOM and Infosys 'issued statements against recruiting people from Satyam'.
- Rishi Das (CareerNet, recruiter): 'about 35,000 or 70 per cent' of the workforce scouting for jobs. A recruiter's estimate.
- A mail from B. Rama Raju of 24 October threatened 'sacking/demotion in cases where 80 per cent of targets are not met' and cost-cutting of variable pay. This is reported by BT from employees; the mail itself is not seen.

## 5. Aadhaar (S0127, 2018 judgment, Sikri J. unless stated)

- p. 25: 'UIDAI was established in the year 2009 by an administrative order i.e. by resolution of the Govt. of India, Planning Commission, vide notification dated January 28, 2009.'
- p. 13: 'Notification dated July 02, 2009 whereby Mr. Nandan Nilekani was appointed as the Chairman of UIDAI for an initial tenure of five years in the rank and status of a Cabinet Minister. He assumed charge on July 24, 2009.' The Prime Minister's Council of UIDAI was constituted on 30 July 2009.
- History (pp. 8-12): Empowered Group of Ministers; at its fourth meeting, on 22 December 2006, 'The numbering format of 11 digits was approved' and the authority was to be created by executive order under the Planning Commission. Page 10 (EGoM); PDS task-force order p. 24.
- Chandrachud J. (dissent): 'On 29 September 2011, the Aadhaar project completed one year. An announcement was made of the generation of ten crore enrolments and of more than 3.75 crore Aadhaar numbers.' By the time the Act was notified, 'UIDAI had generated about 100 crore Aadhaar numbers' (citing PIB). The CAG (register) gives 29 September 2010 at Tembhli for the launch; consistent with the 'completed one year' on 29 September 2011.
- The Aadhaar Act 2016 was passed as a Money Bill (petitioners' challenge, p. 94; majority upheld). Bill No. 47 of 2016.

Holdings (majority, conclusions):
- Section 33(2) (national security disclosure) 'struck down' (p. 567 area).
- 'Retention of data beyond the period of six months is impermissible. Therefore, Regulation 27 ... which provides archiving a data for a period of five years is struck down' (p. 558).
- 'some portion of Section 57 as offending and declared that unconstitutional' (p. 282). The portion allowing a 'body corporate or person' to use Aadhaar under 'any contract': check the exact wording in the conclusions.
- The bank-account linking rule 'does not meet the test of proportionality and is also violative of right to privacy of a person which extends to banking details' (p. 512).
- The DoT circular of 23 March 2017 'mandating linking of mobile number with Aadhaar is held to be illegal and unconstitutional as it is not backed by any law and is hereby quashed' (p. 567).

Chandrachud J. (dissent): 'Dignity and rights of individuals cannot be based on algorithms or probabilities. Constitutional guarantees cannot be subject to the vicissitudes of technology.' He would have struck down the Act as not a Money Bill (dissent conclusions; paragraph numbers to confirm).

**2017 privacy judgment (order of the Court).**
- 'The decision in M P Sharma which holds that the right to privacy is not protected by the Constitution stands over-ruled.'
- Kharak Singh likewise over-ruled 'to the extent that it holds that the right to privacy is not protected'.
- 'The right to privacy is protected as an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 and as a part of the freedoms guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution.'

## 7. Thesis links

**Satyam.** The fraud was in the books, not the engineering. Clients 'continue to be happy with Satyam's excellent service delivery' (Karnik, S0279), and the anonymous Europe consultant said the client wanted the team kept together. The scandal showed that the industry's asset was its people and client relationships, while its weak point was governance by promoter-founders and auditors who 'relinquished control' of confirmations (S0280). Engineering work survived the fraud: the company was sold at Rs 58 a share within three months.

**Aadhaar.** IT-industry methods (scale, process, vendor ecosystems) applied to the state. The Supreme Court's 2018 limits (data retention, private use, mandatory linking) are the first judicial limits on what Indian software could be made to do to Indians, as opposed to for foreign clients. Chandrachud's line on 'algorithms or probabilities' links forward to the AI chapters.
