**Condition in the outline.** The opening fresher must be a named person in published reporting, or a documented case in a filed complaint. **No fresher named by their real name was found.** The best documented individual is 'Gayatri (name changed)', a pseudonym given by the reporter Balakrishna Ganeshan (S0311). She is one real, reported person, not a composite. Recommendation to the author: open on her, and state in the first paragraph that the name is the publication's pseudonym. If the author prefers the stricter reading, open instead on the documented public moment of 13-14 February 2025, when the Union Labour Ministry told Karnataka to act on the Mysuru terminations (S0133). ## 1. Summary of findings (c. 300 words)

**The wait.** In 2022 Infosys made campus offers to engineering graduates it then did not bring in. NITES, a union of IT workers based in Pune, complained to the Union Labour Ministry in August 2024 about more than 2,000 such recruits (S0295). Infosys called the allegations 'patently false' (S0315). On 2-3 September 2024, according to NITES, the company issued about 2,000 offer letters with joining dates in October (S0311, S0315).

**Mysuru.** The recruits joined the Mysuru training campus in October 2024 as apprentices. In February 2025 Infosys let go of those who had failed internal assessments three times: about 300 by its count, about 700 by NITES's (S0312, S0133). More rounds followed in March and April, 'almost 800' in all by one count (S0314).

**One trainee.** *Newslaundry*/TNM reported the case of 'Gayatri' (a changed name), aged 24, from Andhra Pradesh. She graduated in 2022, waited nearly two years, joined in October 2024 and signed a 'mutual separation agreement' on 7 February 2025. 'They wasted two and a half years of my career,' she said (S0311).

**The company.** Infosys said every fresher gets three attempts and that the process 'has been in existence for over two decades' (S0311, S0133).

**The state.** The Union Labour Ministry twice told Karnataka to act (S0295, S0133). Karnataka's Labour Department reportedly found no layoff had occurred, because apprentices are not employees (S0314).

**The industry.** At the same time the industry kept growing on paper. Nasscom reported $315 billion in revenue for FY26 (S0306). TCS onboarded 24,000 freshers in the first half of FY27 (S0138, S0310).

**Why this opening.** The prologue sets one person's wait against those totals. The question it raises (whether AI caused the slump, M30) is answered only in Chapter 19, where the evidence shows that delays began in 2022, before agentic AI tools.

## 3. Evidence notes

### 3.1 The wait, 2022-2024

The prologue's subject is a joining date: the day a graduate who has accepted an offer is told to report for work. For most of the industry's history the date followed the offer within months. From 2022 it stopped doing so at several large firms. Aakanksha Chaturvedi of *Business Today* reported in October 2022 that freshers placed by Accenture in November 2021, with offers in April 2022, had seen their joining dates pushed into 2023 (S0134). One had packed her bags two days before being told. These freshers were anonymous.

Infosys's case is the best documented, because a union took it to the government. NITES complained to the Union Ministry of Labour and Employment on 20 August 2024 about more than 2,000 campus recruits from 2022-23 who had not been onboarded (S0295; S0311). The ministry's Regional Labour Commissioner wrote to the Karnataka government on 3 September 2024 (S0295). According to NITES, as reported by *Newslaundry*/TNM, Infosys then issued about 2,000 offer letters on 2 and 3 September 2024 with joining dates in October (S0311). *Deccan Herald*'s Sonal Choudhary reported on 2 October 2024 that 'the remaining 500 are yet to receive a confirmation'. Infosys had told the state labour department that the union's allegations were 'patently false' and that the graduates had received joining dates (S0315).

Two details in that reporting matter for the prologue. First, the recruits were asked to do unpaid online 'pre-training' and assessments in July and August 2024, before they were employees (S0315; S0311). Second, NITES's wider figure, reported by *HR Katha* in June 2024, was that over 10,000 graduates were waiting for joining dates at Wipro, Infosys, TCS, Zensar and LTIMindtree (S0133). That is a union's estimate and must be labelled as such. The same piece reports that Infosys hired 11,900 graduates in FY24 against 50,000 the year before, citing other reports. Infosys's own FY2024 20-F gives 26,975 offers (S0291), a different measure.

### 3.2 One trainee: 'Gayatri'

Balakrishna Ganeshan's report, published on *Newslaundry* on 12 February 2025, is built on interviews with four terminated trainees (S0311). The text says 'TNM spoke to four trainees', so it appears to be a *News Minute* report carried by Newslaundry; the original TNM URL should be found for the citation. The central figure is introduced as '24-year-old Gayatri (name changed)'. Everything known about her comes from this one report. It must be attributed to her, through the reporter, every time.

According to the report:
- **The wait.** Gayatri graduated in 2022 and cleared Infosys's interview for a system engineer post that year. She 'was left hanging for nearly two years before finally being onboarded in October 2024, that too only after numerous emails and a legal intervention' (the reporter's words). From July 2024 she took part in unpaid online pre-training. 'Pre-training was unheard of earlier,' she said (S0311). She then failed the post-training assessment.
- **The summons.** On Thursday 6 February 2025 she received an email summoning her to a meeting the next day. It said: 'You are required to maintain confidentiality, hence do not discuss this, or share this calendar with anyone' (S0311).
- **The meeting.** On 7 February her phone and bag were taken by security staff at the campus entrance, and an HR staffer asked her to sign a 'mutual separation agreement'. Its text, as quoted, read: 'You have knowingly and voluntarily chosen to enter into this agreement and have not been forced or pressured in any way to sign it.' Her account: 'I signed it unwillingly. I did not want to quit the company. They wasted two and a half years of my career. It was my dream job' (S0311; under 50 words if cut after 'career').
- **Leaving.** Trainees were bused back to their accommodation and given until 5 pm to leave. She booked a hotel for the night before travelling home to Andhra Pradesh (S0311). The compensation offered, according to the report, was one month's salary, 'a meager Rs 25,000' (the reporter's words).

The report also quotes two other pseudonymous trainees. 'Raju' said: 'We were thrown on the road' (S0311). 'Rohan' alleged the pass mark had risen to 65% from 50% in earlier years (S0311). It notes that the trainees TNM spoke to denied the allegation, made elsewhere, that bouncers had been hired. That is useful: the reporter recorded a detail that cut against the union's version. *Outlook Business* reported NITES's claim of 'the presence of bouncers and security personnel' (S0312). The book should give both versions.

The book may use Gayatri's account only as hers. It must say plainly that 'Gayatri' is the reporter's pseudonym, and that nothing about her is known beyond the report. It must not add thoughts, settings or dialogue.

### 3.3 The company's account

Infosys's statement, quoted by both *Newslaundry*/TNM and *Deccan Herald*, is the other side and should be given in full or close to it (S0311, S0133): 'At Infosys, we have a rigorous hiring process where all freshers, after undergoing extensive foundational training at our Mysuru campus, are expected to clear internal assessments. All freshers get three attempts to clear the assessment, failing which they will not be able to continue with the organisation, as is also mentioned in their contract. This process has been in existence for over two decades and ensures a high quality of talent availability for our clients.' That runs over 50 words, so the book must quote a part and paraphrase the rest.

The termination email used in the April round, quoted by *Republic Business* and by Moneycontrol via *Business Today*, said the trainee had 'not met the qualifying criteria in the "Generic foundation training program" despite the additional preparation time, doubt-clearing sessions, several mock assessments and three attempts' (S0313, S0314). Infosys offered outplacement help, sponsored courses through NIIT and UpGrad, and a route into Infosys BPM. About 250 trainees enrolled in upskilling and about 150 in outplacement, as reported (S0314).

The Mysuru campus and its training programme run through the book. Chapter 12 describes how the companies built training into the pipeline because engineering colleges did not teach what they needed. On the company's account, the assessment is that pipeline's quality control. On the trainees' account, the assessment was made harder to reduce a batch the company no longer wanted. *Outlook Business* reports that the syllabus and pass mark changed in 2024 (two tests, F1 in Java and FA2 in databases, a 65% threshold, 200 hours of material) (S0312). It also cites Moneycontrol for a rise in termination rates from under 10% to 30-40% (S0312). Anonymous current Infosys employees told *Outlook Business* that 'an excess of resources and fewer projects' lay behind it (S0312). The trainees' own figures are that of 930 who joined on 7 October, 160 passed at the first attempt and 140 at the second (S0311). That is a claim, not a company figure.

### 3.4 The numbers and the state

How many were let go is disputed. Infosys said about 300 in February; NITES said about 700 (S0312, S0133). *Business Today*, relaying Moneycontrol, counts about 300 in February, about 35 in March, 240 on 18 April and 195 later that month, 'almost 800' since February (S0314). *Republic Business* gives 370 for 18 April (S0313).

The legal question was whether they were employees. *Republic* reports that the trainees had joined under the National Apprenticeship Training Scheme (S0313). The Union Labour Ministry directed Karnataka on 13 February 2025 to take 'urgent necessary actions to resolve the dispute' (S0133). But the Karnataka Labour Department, according to a source quoted by Moneycontrol, found that 'They were all apprenticeship trainees, not employees', so retrenchment law did not apply (S0314; the source is unnamed and the order was not seen). NITES filed a further complaint with the skills ministry, arguing that the apprenticeship law had itself been broken (S0313). No outcome was found. The book must not say the trainees were wronged in law. It can say that the state's two levels disagreed about whether the question was even a labour matter.

This links to Chapter 18. There, Karnataka's exemption of IT firms from the Standing Orders Act (S0132) is part of why the industry's workers stood outside much of India's labour law.

### 3.5 The same years, from the top

The prologue should set the trainee's story against the industry's own numbers, without implying that one caused the other.

**Nasscom.** It reported in February 2026 that the tech industry earned $315 billion in FY26, up 6.1%, with 59.5 lakh direct employees and 1.35 lakh net additions (S0306, an industry body's figures).

**Infosys.** Its FY2026 annual report gives 87,286 offers of employment and 5,884,480 applications (S0297).

**TCS.** It told PTI in April 2026 that it had made 25,000 fresher offers for FY27 and that 'Clarity on demand will lead to more hiring' (S0309). By October 2026 about 14,000 had joined in the first quarter and 10,000 in the second (S0310, S0138). Its CHRO Sudeep Kunnumal told analysts on 8 October 2026: 'We are hiring for demand, and we definitely see good demand for these technologies, so we will continue to hire' (S0138; POST-CUTOFF). Its headcount was 598,056, up 4,258 in the quarter (S0138, S0310).

These figures complicate any story in which the factory simply stopped. In 2026 the largest firm's freshers were joining, mostly on schedule. The 2022-24 waits fell in the post-pandemic bust that Chapter 18 documents, before agentic coding tools were in wide use. That is why the outline ties the prologue to myth M30. The book should state that the joining date became uncertain before the machines arrived, and leave Chapter 19 to weigh how much AI has added since.

### 3.6 How to write the opening honestly

The outline's honesty rules govern this scene more than any other, because it is the book's first page. The permitted material is:
- dates, the email's wording, the agreement's wording and Gayatri's quoted sentences, all from one report (S0311);
- the company's statement (S0311, S0133);
- the union's figures, labelled (S0312, S0133);
- the state's actions (S0295, S0133, S0314).

The scene cannot include:
- what Gayatri thought or felt beyond what she is quoted as saying;
- the weather, the room or the HR staffer's words, none of which are reported;
- her real name.

A plain opening that names the pseudonym, gives the dates and quotes her is both legal and honest, and strong enough without invention. The final drafting rule is that the first mention reads something like 'a trainee the reporter called Gayatri'.

## 6. Disputes

- **(ggg)** The October 2024 Infosys joining date: 7 October (S0311) vs 21 October (S0313). There may have been two batches.
- **(hhh)** TCS's fresher numbers. *Business Standard* frames 25,000 as the FY27 target against a usual 40,000 (S0139). *Business Today* reports 25,000 offers made by April with more to depend on demand, and 'reportedly' over 44,000 hired in FY26 (S0309). Treat 25,000 as offers made by April, not a cap. Ch. 19 and M30 to be adjusted at drafting.
- **(iii)** The 18 April 2025 Infosys round: 240 (S0314) vs 370 (S0313).
- **(jjj)** The deadline to leave campus on 7 February 2025: 5 pm (S0311) vs 6 pm (reported from *Deccan Herald* by other outlets; DH page not read).
- **(kkk)** TCS Q2 FY27 headcount date: *Business Standard* says 30 June 2026, an error; *Business Today* gives the September quarter (S0138, S0310).
