# Working notes: Epilogue, India in 2035 (2026-2035)

**Status:** step 1 notes, 2026-10-12. Feeds `epilogue.md`. Every forward-looking number below is someone's projection and is labelled with whose. All items were live-checked on 2026-10-12.

## Sources read

**New S0316.** NITI Aayog (Frontier Tech Hub), with Nasscom and BCG. *Roadmap for Job Creation in the AI Economy*. October 2025 (released 10 October 2025 per press). 28-page PDF, read in the relevant parts. Printed page = PDF page − 9 from PDF p. 17 (printed 8) onwards. Forewords on PDF pp. 4-5 are unnumbered.

Projections and statements:
- **Foreword, Debjani Ghosh (PDF p. 5):** 'By 2031, India's technology sector stands at a crossroads: we could lose 1.5 million jobs or create up to 4 million new opportunities.' This sits under a government think-tank's name, co-written with the industry lobby (Nasscom) and a consultancy (BCG). Ghosh was formerly Nasscom's president.
- **Executive summary (PDF p. 6), business as usual:** 'the headcount in the tech services sector could go down from 7.5 to 8 million in 2023 to 6 million by 2031'. CX: from 2-2.5 million to 1.8 million. With action: tech up to 10 million and CX 3.1 million.
- **Definition (PDF p. 21, printed 12):** 7.5-8 million is the 'total tech workforce' and includes tech jobs in other sectors. It is not comparable with Nasscom's 5.8 million industry headcount (S0140).
- **Internal inconsistency (PDF p. 11):** 'by 2030, we can add 2 million more new jobs' vs 10 million by 2031 elsewhere.
- **Printed p. 13 (PDF p. 22):**
  - 'We estimate the IT Services industry to grow 3-9% annually'.
  - 'we expect the price-per-FTE for the industry to grow 5-8% annually over the next few years'. This contrasts with the 'AI deflation' reported by firms and brokers in 2026 (S0142); see dispute (lll).
- **Printed p. 8 (PDF p. 17), three 'archetypes of delivery pyramids':**
  - scale shrinks proportionately;
  - productivity gains at the bottom layer;
  - lower layers 'substantially reduced' with a 'human in the loop'.

  Quote: 'In an IT services company, this typically translates to a shrinking of entry-level roles involving QA and support'. And: 'In short, AI boosts productivity across the board. This means fewer layers, faster releases, and leaner teams.'
- **Printed p. 7 (PDF p. 16): 'Rekha' (junior QA engineer, Pune) and 'Aman' (L1 support agent, Chennai)** are ILLUSTRATIVE PERSONAS in a policy report, not reported people. NEVER quote or present them as real. If they are mentioned at all, say they are the report's invented examples.
- **Printed p. 10 (PDF p. 19):**
  - AI talent supply is '50% of the current demand' (citing Nasscom).
  - Demand rises from 800,000-850,000 to over 1,250,000 in 2024-26.
  - Net talent migration of -1.55 per 10,000.
- **Printed p. 9 (PDF p. 18):** India's share of granted AI patents fell from 8-10% (2010) to under 5% (2023), citing the 2025 AI Index.
- **Proposes an 'India AI Talent Mission':** 'To position India as the global epicentre of AI talent by 2035' (printed p. 14, PDF p. 23). No adoption by government was found.

## Disputes found
- **(lll) Price per worker.** NITI/BCG/Nasscom expect price per FTE to grow 5-8% a year (S0316, printed p. 13). Firms and brokers in 2026 describe AI deflation, with deal values down by about a fifth for the same scope (S0142). Different dates (2025 vs 2026) and different measures (price per FTE vs deal value), but opposite in direction.
- **(mmm) Tech workforce base.** NITI's 7.5-8 million (2023, all tech jobs in all sectors) vs Nasscom's 5.67-5.8 million (industry only) (S0316, S0140). Never compare them directly.
- **(nnn) NITI's upside year.** NITI's own report gives the upside as '2 million more new jobs' 'by 2030' (PDF p. 11) and up to 4 million by 2031 elsewhere (S0316).
