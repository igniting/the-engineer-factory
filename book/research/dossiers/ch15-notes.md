## 1. The first wave (1985-1999)

**TI (S0061).** '1985 TI begins operation in India as the first multinational to establish a software design center in India and the first to install and use a satellite dish for exporting software.'
- 1989: mixed-signal IC design centre.
- 1995: hard-disk-drive DSP cores.
- 2000: 3G and WLAN chipsets.
- 2003: *Business World* names TI India 'Best Company to Work for in India'.
- 2004: seven-acre Bangalore facility.

Ch. 8 and 10 cover TI's arrival (the earth station and the '25 rules'). Here the point is continuity: a 20-year engineering centre doing chip design, not services.

**HP (S0097, pp. 62-64).** Radha Basu set up HP's Bangalore software centre in the mid-1980s; it had 400 people by 1989 (Ch. 13).

**Motorola (S0199).** Company claim: CMM Level 5 in 1993 (Ch. 11).

**American Express and GE (S0206).** Captive back offices from the late 1980s and 1996 (Ch. 14).

**Microsoft (S0195, 24 March 1998).**
- Plan for a 'software development center in Hyderabad', operational in the second half of 1998, 'Subject to clearance by regulatory agencies'.
- Focus: 'interoperability of the Microsoft Windows NT operating system and the BackOffice family with non-Microsoft platforms'.
- 'The development center initially will employ about 25 people.'
- Paul Maritz: 'The majority of our development work will always be based in the United States, but we have a great deal of faith in the IT skills of India. Although this center is initially relatively small, the work will be highly valued and strategically important'.
- Moshe Dunie: 'The development center in India will be an extension to our product team in Redmond'.
- Srini Koppolu, nine years at Microsoft, relocated from Redmond as general manager, reporting to S. Somasegar.
- The decision followed 'a long evaluation process that began over a year ago'.
- Register: it started August 1998 in temporary premises and moved to HITEC City in February 1999 with 36 staff (release not read).

**Oracle 1994, SAP Labs 1998, Intel 1999.** Register notes only (S0196, S0197, S0198). Not read; do not quote.

**Cognizant: the captive that became a services firm (S0274, 1998).**
- 'The Company began its software development and maintenance services business in early 1994 as an in-house technology development center for The Dun & Bradstreet Corporation and its operating units.'
- In November 1996 it was spun off with other D&B units to form Cognizant Corporation, the parent.
- At 31 March 1998: about 270 staff in North America ('15 of whom are United States citizens or permanent residents'), about 30 in Europe, and 'approximately 870 persons ... in its offshore software development centers in India'. That is about 74% of its 'technical professionals'.
- 'wages in India are increasing at a faster rate than in the United States'.

Key point: one of the largest 'Indian-model' firms began as an American company's captive centre. The line between captive and services firm was porous from the start. The 15 US citizens or residents out of 270 North American staff show that even the onsite staff were mostly Indian visa holders.

## 2. Scale (2000s)

**Cognizant.**
- 31 December 2002 (S0275): 1,425 in North America, 240 in Europe, 'approximately 4,500 persons ... in its offshore IT development centers in India'.
- 31 December 2007 (S0276): about 55,400 in total, about 9,800 in North and South America; 'a majority of our technical professionals were located' in India.
- End of 2012 (S0277): about 124,100 in Asia Pacific, 27,300 in the Americas and 5,300 in Europe (156,700 in total).
- End of 2024 (S0192): 'approximately 336,800 employees ... with 241,500 in India, 42,800 in North America' (72% in India).
- S0254 Table 1: Cognizant was the largest H-1B sponsor in 2012 (9,281).

**Accenture (S0193, 29 January 2007).** 'Accenture will have more staff in India than in any other country by the end of August.'
- 35,000 in India by 31 August 2007, 'up 52 percent', against 'just over 30,000' in the US (William D. Green, CEO, in Bangalore).
- Global Delivery Network about 65,000; total headcount 160,000.
- Green: 'There is more outsourcing work than we can do'.

**IBM (S0194, 1 February 2007).**
- 'The technology company's Indian workforce now stands at 53,000, up from 39,000 a year ago and 23,000 two years ago.'
- Projection of more than 70,000 in 2007, 'almost 25% of its current worldwide workforce of 300,000'.
- 'IBM workers in India are engaged in everything from routine coding to high-end system design.'
- Bangalore consolidates service-oriented-architecture software development.
- Counterpoint to IBM's 1978 exit (Ch. 5).

**Google (S0200, AP, 13 October 2004).** Brin in Bangalore: 'One approach we are taking is that Bangalore is where we run a mirror exactly of what we have in the United States in terms of development.' Google 'was in fact "too late" in tapping Indian talent. "We would have preferred to do it sooner."' Offices in Bangalore and Hyderabad 'earlier this year'. Founders seeking 'extraordinary talented entrepreneurial people who want to make a big change in the world'. Work: speech-based and multilingual search, personalisation, Gmail features. Krishna Bharat's role is in the register only (IIT Kanpur seminar listing); not in this article.

**Multinational R&D centres (S0207 †, Zinnov via EE Times, 17 March 2008).**
- 'There are an estimated 594 multinational R&D centers in India employing 146,760 workers.'
- Bangalore 312, Pune 96, New Delhi area 87, Hyderabad 55, Chennai 39.
- 115 in telecom and networking, 75 in semiconductors and EDA, 48 in consumer electronics.
- 'Ninety-three new centers were established in 2004, but that total dropped to just 24 in 2007.'
- 283 centres employed more than 100 people.
- Annual attrition 8-20%. Pari Natarajan (Zinnov CEO): productivity at smaller centres up to 40% lower.
- Headline: 'India's R&D drive slows'. Consultant data.

**Captives in BPO (S0278, manuscript p. 14).** 'Consolidation is also eroding the distinction between "captives" (BPO firms owned by U.S. client firms such as Dell, EDS, and HP that service only their owners) and "third-party firms"'. Software firms started or bought BPO arms: Wipro Spectramind, Infosys Progeon, TCS bought Phoenix, IBM acquired Daksh.

On careers (p. 21): 'captive call centers are able to provide more promotions to higher levels than "third-party" call centers, because the market stability they enjoy fosters longer career paths'. Turnover in five captives was 15-20% 'although even some captives reported rates as high as 30%', against an average of 30% (range 12-62%) in seven large third-party centres (Remesh 2004) (p. 19).

This is the only comparative evidence in hand on careers in captives vs third parties.

## 3. GCCs (2010s-2024)

**S0141 † (Zinnov page, 11 September 2024).**
- 'over 1,700 Global Capability Centers (GCCs) and more than 2,975 units as of FY2024'.
- 'These centers have collectively generated $64.6 billion in revenue and employ over 1.9 million professionals.'
- 'over 400 new GCCs' in the five years.
- 'nearly one-third of their global Engineering activities now based in the country'.
- 'over 500 centers now dedicated to AI and Machine Learning'; 'Over 185 GCCs have established dedicated AI/Machine Learning Centers of Excellence'; 'a talent pool exceeding 120,000 AI professionals'.
- 'more than 6,500 global roles ... including over 1,100 women leaders'.
- Tier II and III cities offer 'potential reductions of 25%-30%' in cost.
- Projection for 2030: 2,100-2,200 centres, 2.5-2.8 million staff, $99-105bn.
- Karnataka policy 'aims to double the number of GCCs to 1,000 and generate 350,000 jobs by 2029'.

Consultant and lobby figures; definitions (what counts as a GCC, 'revenue' as cost-plus transfer pricing) need care.

**Comparison.** 1.9 million in GCCs (FY2024) against about 5.95 million in the whole tech industry in FY2026 (NASSCOM, S0140). Roughly a third of India's tech workforce works for 'the other employers'. The years differ, so the comparison is approximate.

**Amazon (S0201, 2019).** Hyderabad campus: 'our first owned campus outside USA and the single largest building in terms of area and head count for Amazon globally'; '15,000 work points across 1.8 million square feet'. Company blog.

**Cognizant 10-K FY2024 (S0192): GCCs now compete with services firms.**
- Competitors include 'clients' in-house technology resources, such as GCCs'.
- 'we face competition from clients' in-house technology resources, such as GCCs, which may provide a lower cost alternative to our services.'
- 'We compete for employees not only with other companies in our industry but also with ... our clients' GCCs.'

The arc: Cognizant began as a captive (1994). Thirty years later, captives are what it fears.

**State policy.** Karnataka GCC Policy 2024-29 (S0204, register; GO not read). Budget 2025-26 framework for GCCs in tier-2 cities (S0205, register; text not read). Both live-checked earlier; not read in the original.

## 4. A parallel profession?

The outline's claim is that GCCs offer different pay, work and career ladders from services firms. Evidence in hand is thin:
- **Brin** (S0200): Bangalore runs 'a mirror' of US development, i.e. the same product work, not services.
- **Maritz** (S0195): 'the majority of our development work will always be based in the United States', i.e. a small, subordinate centre at first.
- **IBM** (S0194): 'everything from routine coding to high-end system design'.
- **Saxenian 2001** (S0255, p. 11): a US multinational's ODC chief, 'Technologically, there's zero difference' (Ch. 10).
- **Kuruvilla and Ranganathan** (S0278): captive call centres had longer career paths and lower turnover.
- **TI India** 'Best Company to Work for' 2003 (S0061; *Business World* award as reported by TI).
- **Zinnov 2024**: 6,500 'global roles' held in Indian GCCs.

No pay data. **Gap:** compensation surveys (e.g. AON or Mercer, by employer type), job-site data, or studies of MNC R&D labour markets. The chapter should not assert a pay premium without evidence.

## 5. M25

Stays FALSE as worded. Add Cognizant's origin as a D&B captive (1994; S0274) and the 2008 Zinnov count (594 centres, 146,760 staff; S0207). 'GCC' is a rebranding of the multinational development centre and captive. What is new is scale (1.9 million), breadth (banks, retailers) and function (global roles, AI).
