# Myths and contested claims

**Chapter numbers in this file refer to outline v0** (until re-tagged in Phase 2). Mapping to outline v1 (2026-10-12): v0 1-14 = v1 1-14; v0 15 (Satyam and Aadhaar) = v1 16; v0 16 (product question) = v1 17; v0 17 (pandemic) = v1 18; v0 18 (AI) = v1 19; new v1 15 (foreign centres/GCCs) draws on sources tagged 14 and 16.

Each claim: where it is commonly stated, what the evidence shows so far, a **provisional** verdict, and source IDs. Verdicts: TRUE / MOSTLY TRUE / MIXED / MOSTLY FALSE / FALSE / UNRESOLVED. All verdicts below are provisional (Phase 1c, 2026-10-11) and must be re-tested in the relevant chapter dossier against the sources themselves (most are still at 'located', not 'seen'). A myth may only be called FALSE in the book when two independent sources support that verdict.

Each entry also notes **what would change the verdict** so the dossier work is targeted.

| M07 | Only 5% (or 25%) of Indian engineers are employable. | MOSTLY FALSE as a statement about the labour market | Figures trace to interested parties. These measure fit to vendor or consultancy benchmarks for specific roles, not employability in the labour-market sense. PLFS graduate unemployment was 13.0% in 2023-24 (S0154). | Independent employer-side or labour-force evidence of skills gaps at similar magnitudes. | S0153, S0154, S0213 | 12, 18 |
| M08 | India produces 1.5 million engineers a year. | MIXED to MOSTLY FALSE | No traceable primary source for 1.5 million graduates; the figure matches approved seats. Duke: c. 215,000 engineering/CS/IT graduates in 2004, of whom 112,000 bachelor's and 103,000 diplomas, against press claims of 350,000; latest official graduation data were from 1993 (S0213, pp. 2-3, 5, 8). Aspiring Minds: 'six hundred thousand' graduates a year c. 2013 (S0153, p. 7, unsourced). Rajaraman's 135,000 (1998) to 1.3 million (2010) is called both 'students' and graduates 'per year' and is probably intake (S0001, pp. 12, 56; dispute ii). | M09 | Indian IT created India's middle class. | MOSTLY FALSE as worded | The sector's direct employment is small relative to India's workforce (c. 5.95 million in FY26 per Nasscom, S0140; earlier estimates put IT-ITES at a very small share of total employment, S0215), and India's middle-class growth has many sources.
